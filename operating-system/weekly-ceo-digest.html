Operating system

Cross-surface patterns: where separate reports point at the same problem

Pattern 1

The pilot problem is a setup problem

  • Three separate reports describe the same failure: value is clear, but nobody on the customer's side owns getting users in.
  • A pilot that never activates reads to the buyer as a product that didn't work.

Where

  • Onboarding: Clearwater pilot at 4 of 25 users in week two
  • Voice of Market: a pilot sponsor and a prospect both raised setup
  • Support: access setup is a growing ticket theme

The move Assign a setup owner on our side to every pilot from day one, and get Clearwater to 15 active users before the readout.

Pattern 2

Two buyers, one deal

  • The largest deal isn't stuck on product; it's stuck on who decides.
  • In a tighter market, a split decision defaults to the smaller, slower option.

Where

  • Pipeline: Talbot & Greer divisions want different scopes
  • Voice of Market: buyers asking for ROI proof earlier in the cycle
  • Market Scan: dealers trimming inventory

The move CEO note to Talbot & Greer's COO proposing one decision owner, and bring a site-by-site ROI model to the next demo so the full rollout can win on numbers.

Pattern 3

Healthy base, honest forecast

  • The base is generating referrals and steady usage.
  • The forecast is the only place the picture is flattering, because one deal is dated for a quarter its budget has left.

Where

  • Usage and support inside normal ranges
  • One new deal from a customer referral
  • Pipeline: Pemberton's budget moved to next year

The move Re-date Pemberton to next fiscal year and keep the quarter's forecast to deals with a dated next step.
